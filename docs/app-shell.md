# The application: welcome page, projects and workspaces

`cargo run --release -p kw-app` starts **Kerfwright**, the one program that ties the engines of Phases 1–5 together, laid out as a conventional ribbon-style CAD application: a light theme, an application bar, a ribbon with tabs and grouped icon buttons, a browser tree, a timeline and a orientation widget. `scripts/app_acceptance.sh` runs the tests and writes a screenshot of every screen to `target/app/`.

## Welcome page
New design (name + folder, saved at once as `<name>.kwf`), Open project (native dialog), Open the demo part, and the ten most recent projects (kept in `~/.config/kerfwright/recent.json`; missing files are marked with a "Forget" button). `kerfwright FILE.kwf`, `--demo` and `--workspace NAME` skip it.

## Project and frame
A project is a `.kwf` file: the feature document of Phase 4 plus the Phase 5 data as extra entries (`cam.cbor`, `fea.cbor`, `board.cbor`, through `kw-store`). **Application bar:** home (back to the welcome page), File, Undo / Redo, Save (Ctrl+S, Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y), the project name with `*` while unsaved; closing, opening another project or quitting asks before discarding changes. **Ribbon:** the workspace menu at the left (DESIGN / MANUFACTURE / SIMULATION / ELECTRONICS / RENDER) and the tabs and tool groups of that workspace.

## Design (Sketch and Model as one workspace)
* **Create Sketch** → the view shows the three origin planes (hover highlights, planar faces highlight too) → click a **plane or a planar face of the body** (curved faces are refused with a message) → the view becomes the flat sketch canvas with the ribbon's **SKETCH** tab (Line, Rectangle, Circle, Arc, Spline, Point; Trim, Extend, Offset; Dimension and the 13 constraints; Finish Sketch / Discard) and a sketch palette at the right with the solver state. When sketching on a face, the body's edges in that plane are drawn as faint reference lines and frame the canvas.
* **Snapping and inference** (as in conventional parametric CAD; the **Snaps** list in the sketch palette and in the ribbon's SNAPS menu switches each kind off): the cursor snaps, in this order, to an existing **point** (square), the **origin** (circle with cross), the **midpoint** of a line (triangle), the **intersection** of two curves (cross), a point **on a curve** (ring), dashed **inference lines**, and finally the **grid** (adaptive spacing). Inference lines are horizontal / vertical and multiples of the **angle step** (15° by default, 5°–90° selectable) from the start of the line being drawn, **parallel / perpendicular** to existing lines, and horizontal / vertical **alignments** with points the cursor has rested on for 0.3 s (marked with a small +); where two lines cross the cursor snaps to the crossing. A label shows length and angle while drawing and the name of the snap. Completing a snap adds the matching **constraint**: midpoint, point-on-curve on both curves at an intersection, a fixed point at the origin, horizontal / vertical / parallel / perpendicular for the line that was inferred (angle, alignment and grid snaps only place geometry). Hold **Alt** to ignore all snaps for one click. Not done: snapping while dragging existing geometry, typing an exact length / angle.
* **How a sketch reads** (as is usual in parametric sketchers): blue lines can still move, **black** lines are fully constrained, red ones conflict (yellow: redundant), **purple** lines are projected from the model (Project / Intersect). Closed outlines are **shaded light blue** (those are the profiles an extrude can use; holes inside are left clear, open curves stay unshaded). Small **constraint icons** sit beside the geometry (horizontal, vertical, parallel, perpendicular, tangent, equal, coincident, midpoint, fix, …), dimensions show in blue or, when only measuring, in grey brackets. The sketch palette's **Display** list switches the grid, profiles, constraints and dimensions on and off. **Drag a box** in empty space to select: left to right takes what lies completely inside, right to left also what the box touches (Shift adds); pan with the middle or right button. Not done: shading of overlapping outlines that cross each other, and the Slice option (the sketch is on a flat canvas, not inside the 3-D model).
* **Finish Sketch** → the **Extrude dialog opens at once** with that sketch, defaulting to *Join* when the sketch lies on a body (*New Body* otherwise). The model shows a **live preview** of the result (cut, join …) as you change distance, direction, symmetric or operation; an arrow in the view drags the distance; OK commits, Cancel discards, a failing preview is shown in red and blocks OK.
* **Regions and sketch reuse** (as in conventional parametric CAD): every curve of a sketch is **cut where it crosses or touches another one** (lines, arcs and circles in closed form; ellipses and splines by sampling refined with Newton's method) and the enclosed areas of the result are the **regions** — circle only, square only, the overlap, the two halves of a circle cut by a line, the arms and the middle of a "+" drawn from lines, a ring with a hole, the shape inside the hole. Free-standing open curves and whiskers belong to no region and are simply ignored. The Extrude dialog draws the regions over the sketch plane and lists them (**Default / All / None**): hover shows the one under the pointer, a click toggles it. The dialog **starts with the usual regions chosen** (everything enclosed, shapes inside holes left as holes) and will not go ahead with nothing chosen ("Click the regions to extrude"); a sketch with a single region needs no click. Chosen neighbours merge into one profile (no wall where the line was), separate pieces are extruded and united. Each Extrude takes its own regions, depth and operation (New Body / Join / Cut / Intersect). A sketch that a feature has used is **hidden** afterwards; its eye in the browser brings it back to make another feature from other regions, and it is shown again whenever a tool that works on it is open. Every feature stays tied to the one sketch, so changing a dimension in it updates them all, and deleting it removes them all. Region choices are saved as the labels of the boundary entities plus a point inside; choices saved by older versions (named by loops) are converted by position when used. Limits: ellipse / spline crossings that only graze (very shallow) can be missed; selected regions that touch only at a corner are extruded as separate pieces and may fail to unite; side-face names of existing features can change when a sketch gains intersections.
* **Tools** (ribbon SOLID tab): Extrude, Revolve, Hole (click a face to place it), Rectangular and Circular pattern, Mirror, Fillet, Chamfer (click edges, Shift adds; edges are hit within 8 px of the pointer, light up orange under it and stay blue once picked, and the view keeps showing the body the picks refer to — the filleted result appears after OK), Shell (click the faces to remove), Combine, Change Parameters (named values usable in any expression), Origin Planes. Each is the same kind of dialog and edits an existing feature too.
* **Browser** (left): Document Settings, Origin (planes with eye toggles), Sketches (eye toggle shows or hides the sketch in 3-D), Bodies, Features. Click selects, double-click edits, right-click offers Edit / Suppress / Roll back here / Delete.
* **Timeline** (bottom): a chip per feature (red when it fails, grey when suppressed), drag to reorder (refused with the engine's message when a feature would come before something it needs), a rollback marker (drag it, or use the |< < > >| buttons), the same context menu. While rolled back, adding features is refused until the timeline is rolled forward.
* **View**: the orientation widget (click a face to turn the camera there, animated), orbit (drag), pan (right/middle drag), zoom (wheel), hover and selection highlights, and a navigation bar (Home, Fit, shaded / wireframe, edges, planes).

## The ribbon (round 2)
Tabs **SOLID, SURFACE, SHEET METAL, FORM, TOOLS** (and **SKETCH** while sketching). Each group is a few big icon buttons plus a `TITLE ▾` dropdown that lists every command of the group, with its icon, name, shortcut and category headings (CREATE: sketch-based / features / pattern; MODIFY; CONSTRUCT: plane / axis / point; INSPECT; ASSEMBLE; …). A command that cannot run says why in its tooltip. The ribbon scrolls sideways when the window is narrow.
* **CREATE ▾** adds Tapped Hole, Thread, Rib, Pipe; **MODIFY ▾** adds Move / Copy, Scale, Split Body (Bodies are listed in the browser with an eye, rename, colour and material; a click makes a body the target of new features).
* **Extrude** has *Start*, *To face*, *Thin* (inside / outside / centred) and, for sketches with overlapping shapes, **region picking**: the regions are drawn over the sketch plane, click to toggle. **Revolve** can turn about a clicked body edge or a construction axis.
* **CONSTRUCT ▾**: planes, axes and points (listed under *Construction* in the browser with eye toggles).
* **INSPECT ▾**: Measure, Interference, **Section Analysis** (plane XY / YZ / XZ or a construction plane, offset slider, flip).
* **ASSEMBLE ▾** (sections COMPONENT, JOINTS, MOTION, INSPECT; primary buttons New Component, Joint, As-Built Joint):
  * **New Component** makes an empty component and opens it for editing; **Component from Body** copies the active body's features into a component (the body is hidden in the design); **Component from Design**; **Insert Component** (a `.kwf`, kept linked to the file: its design, or its assembly as a sub-assembly) and **Insert Copy** (no link); **Activate / Finish Component** edit a component in place (double-click it in the tree or in the view). While a component is open the browser shows *Editing <name>* with a Finish button and the design view shows that component.
  * **Joint**: click a point on each of two components (a flat face gives its centre, corners and edge middles; a round face its axis; a circle its centre), choose the type (Rigid, Revolute, Slider, Cylindrical, Planar, Ball), Flip, offset, angle and motion limits; the first component moves to the second at once (preview) and OK makes the joint. **As-Built Joint** joins two components where they are. **Rigid Group** makes components move as one. **Ground** grounds or releases the selected components (several may be grounded).
  * **Assembly View** poses every component; click selects (Shift adds), drag moves (a free component slides in the plane facing the camera, one on a joint drives the joint within its limits), double-click opens it. **Move Component** types a shift and a turn. **Drive Joints** has a slider per degree of freedom.
  * **Interference** lists overlaps, outlines them in red and can re-check after every change; **Bill of Materials** lists parts with quantity, material (pick one per part), volume, mass and size, and exports CSV.
  * The **browser** has *Components* (eye, a **G** badge on grounded ones, sub-assemblies indented; right-click: Activate, Ground, Rename, Copy, Isolate, Show all, Create rigid group, Group into sub-assembly, Delete), *Joints* (double-click for properties: type, values, limits, flip, offset, angle) and *Rigid Groups*.
  * **LINKS** (insert copy, Update Links, Auto-Update): a linked component carries a badge in the browser (**L** up to date, amber **!** its file changed, red **?** its file is missing, red **!** unreadable) and a right-click menu (Update from file, Link to another file, Break link). The files are looked at when the project opens and about every second; the project keeps a copy, so a missing file never stops it from opening. A linked component cannot be edited in place until its link is broken.
  * **Mechanisms**: a joint that closes a kinematic loop is marked *solved* (tick *Solved by a loop* in its properties to change that): Drive Joints shows its value but does not offer a slider, shows the mechanism's degrees of freedom, and **Animate** moves a driven joint through its range. Dragging any link of a loop turns the driven input; a position the mechanism cannot reach is explained instead of being drawn.
  * Undo / Redo act on the assembly while the assembly view is shown and on the design otherwise.
* **SURFACE**: CREATE ▾ Extrude, Revolve, Sweep, Loft (each opens the solid dialog set to *Output: Surface*), **Patch** and **Ruled Surface** (click the edges; Shift adds); MODIFY ▾ **Trim** (plane or a clicked face, side to keep), **Extend**, **Match** (click the patch's open edge, then the face to meet; G1 or G2), **Stitch** (nothing picked takes every surface), **Unstitch**, **Thicken**, **Offset Surface**; INSPECT ▾ **Zebra**, **Curvature**, **Draft** (a window sets the kind, direction, density or range; a colour bar shows the scale) and **Edge Continuity** (coloured edges and a window of counts). Commands that need a surface say so when there is none.
* **SKETCH ▸ CREATE ▾ Text**, **MODIFY ▾ Rectangular / Circular Pattern** (select curves first), **PROJECT ▾ Project / Intersect**.
* **SHEET METAL**: L / U / Z strips or a custom strip (thickness, K-factor, radius, width, flats and bends), **Flat Pattern** window with bend table, DXF export and Refold. **FORM**: box, cylinder, sphere, torus cages (cage drawn in the view; OK finishes it into a body). **TOOLS**: inspect, appearance, parameters, import / export.

## Files, more tools and measuring
* **File ▸ Import STEP / IGES / STL…** adds the solids of a file as an `Import` feature (the file's text is kept in the project, so the `.kwf` stays self-contained); a file without a usable solid changes nothing and the reason is shown. An STL file first opens an *Import mesh* window (units, y-up, and what the file holds: triangles, vertices, closed or open edges); *Import* adds an `ImportMesh` feature, a mesh body listed in Bodies as “(mesh)”, drawn smooth shaded with lines along edges sharper than 30° and open boundaries, selected by clicking it (a CPU ray test; the GPU pick buffer does not see meshes). Right-click it in Bodies ▸ **Convert to solid** adds a `MeshToSolid` feature after trying it on a copy. Measure shows a mesh body's triangles, area, volume, mass, centre of mass and inertia; Export writes mesh bodies into STL / OBJ / GLB. **File ▸ Export** writes the model's solid as STEP, STL, OBJ (+ MTL) or GLB.
* **Sketch shapes** (ribbon SHAPES group): polygon (sides in the ribbon), slot, ellipse, 3-point arc, 3-point circle, fit spline; **Fillet / Chamfer** (click the point where two lines meet; size in the ribbon), **Mirror** (click the entities, Enter, click the line). Half-drawn shapes show a dashed preview. Splines and ellipses are valid profiles for every solid tool.
* **Extrude** has *Extent* (Distance / Through all), *Second side*, *Taper angle*; **Hole** has *Through all*. Combinations the kernel cannot do (a taper with a symmetric extent or a second side) are reported in the dialog.
* **MORE** group: Loft, Sweep, Draft, Variable fillet, Offset body; **CONSTRUCT ▸ Plane** (an origin plane moved and tilted; sketch on it from the browser).
* **Measure** (INSPECT): pick faces and edges (two picks are compared) and read areas, lengths, radii, distances and angles; the body's volume, area, mass in a chosen material, centre of mass, bounding box and inertia.
* The full status against a conventional workflow is in `docs/reference-workflow-comparison.md`.

## Other workspaces (same frame, own panels)
**MANUFACTURE** (CAM): New Setup, Libraries, Face, Pocket, 2D / Inside Contour (Adaptive Clearing appears only in builds with the `adaptive-clearing` feature), Chamfer, Drill, Bore, 3D Finish, Waterline, Rest Finish, Swarf, Tilted Contour and Simulate in the ribbon; setups (work coordinates, stock, body roles), operation settings, simulation results and the G-code post (library posts, per-setup programs, setup sheet) in the left panel. **SIMULATION** (FEA): Solve and result colouring; supports and loads on faces picked in the view. **ELECTRONICS** (ECAD): sample board, Place, Route All, Check, Pour, fabrication files, STEP; layers and violations in the panel. **RENDER**: Realtime / Path Trace, Denoise, Save PNG.

**DRAWING**: left panel lists sheets with their views and annotations; the middle is the sheet (wheel zooms, middle / right drag pans, drag a view or an annotation to move it, Delete removes the selection); the right panel edits the title block and whatever is selected; sheet tabs at the bottom. Ribbon groups SHEET, VIEWS, ANNOTATE, TABLES, MODIFY (Update, Delete View) and OUTPUT (PDF, DXF). Click tools (Section, Detail, dimensions, notes, symbols, Balloon) show a live preview, snap to the model's points and edges and are cancelled with Esc. The ribbon's Update is lit and the sheet shows a banner when the model has changed since the views were made. Undo / Redo apply to the drawings while this workspace is shown.

## What is verified, and what is not
* `crates/kw-app/tests/shell.rs` (53 tests in all; 26 in the first version): the shell tests of the first version, plus the design flows: sketch on a plane → straight into a previewed Extrude → OK (volume 3000); pick a planar face → sketch on it → Cut with the dialog → volume 3000 − π·3²·2; a curved face is refused; Escape / Discard leave nothing behind; timeline reorder accepted and refused, rollback and roll-forward, suppress, edit-in-place of an extrude and of its sketch (the downstream volume follows), delete refused when something depends on a feature, adding refused while rolled back; chamfer, shell, hole and rectangular pattern against closed-form volumes; revolve against π(R²−r²)h; orientation-widget mapping and camera interpolation; the plane under the pointer; a triangle drawn with the origin, horizontal and 135° snaps extruding to the exact volume. Snap logic itself: `crates/kw-sketch/tests/snaps.rs` (13 tests: midpoint, intersection, origin, polar angles, parallel / perpendicular, alignment, grid, priorities, random clicks never conflict) and `crates/kw-sketcher/tests/ui.rs` (dwell-to-acquire, Alt, every marker draws).
* Screenshots come from the real application through eframe, so the GPU path into egui, the light theme and the layout are checked on every screen listed in `scripts/app_acceptance.sh`.
* New in this round: `exchange` (import / export, STL volume from triangles, STEP round trip), ellipse / spline extrudes, extents and taper (closed forms), loft / sweep / draft / offset, hole through all, variable fillet, measure, snapping, `kw-sketch/tests/shapes.rs`.
* **Not tested here:** real mouse and keyboard interaction in a window (clicking ribbon buttons, dragging chips and the extrude arrow, orbiting) and the native file dialogs. The logic behind every click is tested through the same code paths (`handle_clicks`, actions, dialogs), but the pointer handling itself needs a hand test.

## Limits
* Fillet / chamfer / shell reference faces by name; a feature added while the model is rolled back is refused rather than inserted at the marker.
* The MESH tab is not shown (nothing behind it); the SURFACE tab is described in `docs/surface-report.md`. The full list of what fell short in round 2 (no pyramid Form, …) is in `docs/reference-workflow-comparison.md`.
* Sketching is on the flat 2-D canvas, not in the 3-D view. The model is evaluated on the UI thread (a very heavy model pauses the window); CAM, FEA, routing, stock simulation and denoising run on background threads.
* CAM regions are the outline of a flat face, the model's footprint or a typed rectangle (curved outlines are faceted at 2 µm); FEA meshes the faceted tessellation of every included body (curved faces are not projected onto the true surface) and stores supports and loads by face tag with a geometric signature of the face (see `docs/reference-workflow-comparison.md`); ECAD offers generated sample boards only; Render uses its own GPU device and a fixed 960×600 image.
* **Form editing in the view.** While the Form dialog is open the cage is drawn over a live preview of its smooth surface (the finished body of a Form being edited is hidden). Press on a vertex, edge or face to select it and drag: the selection slides in the plane facing the camera, or along an axis (X / Y / Z) or in the plane across it (Shift+X / Y / Z; the same key again frees it). Shift-click toggles, Shift-drag in empty space draws a box, a click in empty space clears. The EDIT CAGE window holds the lock, grid snap, mirror symmetry (across YZ / XZ / XY through the origin), Sharp / Smooth / Toggle for the edges between the selected vertices, and Undo / Redo; Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y act on the cage while the dialog is open. A dragged cage becomes a custom cage (a later size change does not rebuild it). The scene centre is held for the whole dialog so the picture does not slide while the preview changes. OK adds or replaces the one `Subd` feature (one undo step).
