# Kerfwright vs. a conventional parametric CAD workflow (Design / Solid): where we are

Status after round 2 (multi-body, extrude options, construction geometry, threads, sketch project / patterns / text, section
analysis, assemblies, sheet metal, Form, and the new ribbon), the Drawing workspace and the **SURFACE** tab with surface analysis. **Done** = in the engine, in the UI and covered by tests against
closed forms. What is still missing is listed at the end.

## The ribbon
Icon buttons grouped as in conventional ribbon-style CAD; every group has a **dropdown (`CREATE ▾`, `MODIFY ▾`, …)** that lists *all* of its commands
with icon, name and shortcut under category headings, and the most used ones are big buttons next to it. Tabs: **SOLID**
(CREATE, MODIFY, CONSTRUCT, INSPECT, ASSEMBLE, SELECT), **SURFACE** (CREATE, MODIFY, INSPECT), **SHEET METAL**, **FORM**, **TOOLS** (inspect, appearance and parameters,
import / export) and, while sketching, **SKETCH** (CREATE, MODIFY, PROJECT, CONSTRAINTS, OPTIONS, FINISH). MESH is not
shown: nothing is behind it. A narrow window scrolls the ribbon sideways.

## What fell short (be aware before relying on it)
* **Modelled threads** (Thread and Tapped Hole dialogs, "Modelled", off by default): single-start ISO/UN profile (P/8 flat, 30° flanks), right- or left-hand, external (a pin) and internal (a drilled hole, or a tapped hole cut directly). The kernel still has no helix: a thread tool is the twisted extrusion of its plane section (`kw-feature/src/thread_geom.rs`), skinned a turn at a time (`kw-model` `sweep` with a twist), and the Boolean meets the old cylinder along its flanks. Checked against `area × length` to 1e-5 for 0.5 to 20 turns, against the cut volumes of a block, a pin and a drilled hole (3e-4), and by classifying points either side of the flank. Limits: no runout, lead-in chamfer or class tolerance; the thread starts at the free end of the face (else half a pitch in from the closed end) and must fit the face; the tool's crest flats sit 0.02 mm clear of the surface they pass, so a tapped hole's bore is the thread's minor diameter less 0.04 mm; a twelve-turn thread takes a few seconds to build and several to measure, mesh or draw, so cosmetic remains the default; very long threads (hundreds of turns) and multi-start threads are not built.
* **Pyramid Form** is not offered: its apex makes the patch fit take over a minute. Smooth cages (sphere) take seconds to finish; the body of a Form is slow to measure (volume integration of many patches).
* Form: the cage can be dragged and its edges creased one by one, but there is no extrude / bridge / insert-edge / delete of cage faces (the topology of the cage is a primitive's), no per-edge crease *amount* (sharp = 100 or smooth), symmetry is across a coordinate plane through the origin only, and picking does not hide what is behind the surface (the cage is see-through; the nearer of two overlapping vertices wins). The live preview is the limit surface sampled after 2–4 subdivision steps (about 10 000 triangles at most): it matches the finished body to a fraction of a percent in volume but is not the fitted patch body, whose deviation and G1 report come only after OK. While the dialog is open the finished body of the Form being edited is hidden (with every later feature built on it). Real mouse dragging could not be tried where this was built: the shell test drives the same code with synthetic pointer events.
* **Thin extrude** outside / centred has mitred (sharp) corners. **Regions**: ellipse / spline crossings that only graze can be missed, regions that touch only at a corner are united by the kernel and may fail, and side-face names of existing features can change when a sketch gains intersections.
* **Assemblies**: the joints are walked as a spanning tree from the grounded components; a joint that closes a loop is a constraint whose values are found numerically (Levenberg-Marquardt from the values the joints hold, so a mechanism moved step by step stays on its branch; a different branch is reached only by moving there or by setting starting values). The loop solver is a finite-difference Newton iteration, fine for linkages of a few links but not a general constraint solver: there are no gears, belts, cams or contacts, joint limits of solved joints stop the mechanism rather than being reasoned about, and near a singular position (a toggle point) the branch can flip. Two grounded components still cannot be joined. Which joint is the input is chosen automatically (the one next to the top of the loop) and can be changed with *Solved by a loop* in the joint's properties. A linked component keeps its connection frames as coordinates: if the upstream model changes shape, the frames stay where they were (Update tells which parts changed, not which frames moved off their geometry), and a joint that no longer fits is refused with the loop or frame error and the update is taken back. A linked component cannot be edited in place (break the link first); auto-update acts only while the Design workspace is open; links are compared by content hash and file time, so touching a file without changing it costs one read, and saving is not atomic, so a file read in the middle of a write shows as unreadable until the next check. **Component from Body** copies the body's features, after which the root design and the component are independent. While a component is open, the design view shows that component alone (the assembly comes back on Finish; other components are not shown dimmed). The assembly view shows components only, not the root design's bodies. The overlap of interfering components is shown as red outlines (there is no transparency in the viewer). Undo in the assembly view is separate from the design's undo. Assembly dragging has no collision or contact. Real mouse dragging could not be tried where this was built: it is tested with synthetic pointer events.
* **Project** locks the curves; it projects every model edge (delete the ones you do not want). **Intersect** is read from the tessellation (lines, circles and arcs recognised, the rest as fit splines).
* **STL import**: the file is read (binary or ASCII; multi-solid ASCII is one mesh), corners closer than 1e-6 of the bounding-box diagonal are welded, degenerate triangles dropped and the winding made consistent and outward. The mesh is a *mesh body*: it can be moved, scaled, measured (area; volume, centre of mass and inertia when closed), exported (STL / OBJ / GLB, not STEP) and shown, but solid features (fillet, hole, shell…), FEA and CAM refuse it with “convert it to a solid first”. **Convert to solid** (right-click the body in Bodies) builds a faceted B-rep of planar faces and straight edges: triangles that lie in one plane (within 2e-6 of the diagonal) become one face and vertices along a straight edge are dropped, so a mesh made from a part with flat faces comes back with those faces; curved parts stay as thousands of triangular faces (no cylinder or sphere fitting). The mesh must be closed (an open one is refused with its count of open edges), at most 150 000 triangles (about 5 s at the top), and several separate pieces are united. Booleans on faceted bodies work for ordinary cases but may be slow or refuse near-degenerate slivers. Units and axes: mm / cm / m / inch, z up or y up. Binary files are read as float32, so coordinates keep about 7 significant digits. Not done: 3MF / OBJ import, mesh repair beyond welding and orienting (no hole filling), reduction, a mesh-to-NURBS fit. IGES import is untested against a real sample. The cut cap in Section Analysis has a one-pixel fringe along silhouettes (edge lines cover it).
* Real mouse interaction (dragging, clicking in the 3-D view) and native file dialogs could not be driven where this was built; the logic behind each click is tested through the same code paths and needs a hand test.

* **CAM** (`kw-cam`, `kw-app/src/cam/`): a project holds **setups**. A setup has its own work coordinate system (origin, tool direction from Top / Bottom / Front / Back / Left / Right or typed, a work offset G54–G59), a stock (block, round bar, explicit block, or what the previous setup left), a role for every body (workpiece, fixture, ignored) taken from the design or the posed assembly, a machine and a post. Operations keep their settings and the hash of what they were made from, so a change of model, tool, stock, work system or of an earlier operation shows as *out of date* and **Regenerate** remakes them; regions come from the outlines of flat faces of the model (with islands) or a rectangle. Operations: face, pocket, outside and inside contour (adaptive clearing exists only behind the off-by-default `adaptive-clearing` feature), chamfer (V-tool; the width on the top edge sets the depth), drill (plain or pecked; holes found from the model's cylindrical faces), helical bore, 3-D raster finish, waterline, rest finishing (only where the stock left by the earlier operations stands more than a threshold over the part), swarf (flank milling of a wall, optional draft) and a contour with the tool leaning against the wall. **Simulation** cuts the stock with every operation using its own tool, compares it with the workpiece (material left, material cut too deep, coloured on the stock surface), and checks tool, shank and holder against the fixtures; a second setup can start from the first one's result. **Libraries** (Libraries window): tools of nine kinds with flutes, material, coating, holder and cutting data per material group, holders, machines (spindle and feed limits, travel, tool-changer size, a post, and the rotary axes of a five-axis machine) and post-processors (your own `.post` files are checked as you type, errors name the line); a built-in set ships, yours live in `~/.config/kerfwright/cam/` (or `$KERFWRIGHT_CONFIG`), and the project embeds copies of the machine, the post and the tools it uses. Speeds and feeds follow the material's surface speed and chip load and are held to the machine's limits; tool numbers are unique across the project. Output: one program per setup under its work offset, and a setup sheet (web page and CSV). A machine with two rotary axes is given **tool-centre-point programs**: a tilted setup (3+2) and the swarf and leaning-contour operations are converted with the inverse kinematics and posted as `Rapid5` / `Feed5` (all five dialects; the report lists the travel of the rotary axes, axis-limit violations and the error of the axes between poses). Limits: the stock simulation checks fixtures with an upright tool only (a tilted tool is not checked against fixtures), the machine housing is not modelled, canned drilling cycles are written out as moves on a rotary machine, the waterline works on single-valued surfaces (undercuts are not seen) and keeps within about 0.03 mm of the surface at a 0.5 mm grid, the swarf wall is a ruled surface between the outline and a mitred offset of it (sharp corners swing the axes), the stock surface is as accurate sideways as the dexel cell (0.25 mm and up), work offsets above G59 are not offered, and turning and additive are not built. Checked against closed forms in `crates/kw-cam/tests`, and end to end in `crates/kw-app/tests/cam_*.rs`.
* **FEA** (`kw-fem`, `kw-app/src/fea*.rs`): a study runs on the included bodies of the design or of the posed assembly, each with a material from the library (`kw-materials`); bodies that touch are bonded by node-to-surface ties with a penalty (a patch test across unrelated meshes is met to about 1%, not exactly; overlapping bodies are refused). Study types: linear static (with safety factor against the yield strength), modal (frequencies, animated mode shapes), steady-state thermal (temperature, flux, convection, heat generation), thermal stress (thermal, then the stress of the expansion), and nonlinear static (large displacements, moderate strain, von Mises plasticity with linear hardening, dead loads, load stepping, load-displacement curve; the plasticity uses the first body's yield strength and tangent modulus). Supports can be fixed, per-axis rollers or prescribed displacements; gravity is a load. Each is checked against closed forms in `crates/kw-fem/tests` and `crates/kw-app/tests/fea_multi.rs` (run those with `--release`, the modal and nonlinear cases are slow in debug). A project holds a **list of studies**. A support or load stays on its face: the study keeps the face's signature (body, centre of the surface, area, normal) beside its tag, finds the face again when the numbering shifts (a body left out or put back, a face added) and follows it through small edits (the centre may move by 2 % of the body's size, the area by 5 %); a face it cannot find is shown as gone, the study does not run until it is removed or put on a face again, and the face comes back by itself if the model does. Not done: results are not saved in the file, no contact other than bonded, no buckling, and a face that moves far in a parametric edit has to be picked again (the signature is geometric, not the kernel's persistent name).
* **Drawings** (details in `docs/drawing-report.md`): section cuts are straight lines (no offset / aligned / broken-out), a detail of a section view and dimensions on section and detail views are not offered, there are no break, auxiliary or ordinate / baseline views, dimensions in an **assembly** drawing are fixed in space (they do not follow the model), GD&T symbols are placed but not checked, no automatic balloons, one parts list per sheet. A section's cut outline is read from the tessellation (curved cut edges are polylines at about 1e-4 of the part size) because the kernel's Boolean cannot split a face that has a hole in it. Mouse flows are driven through synthetic pointer events in the tests; a hand test with a real mouse is still due.
* **Surfaces** (details in `docs/surface-report.md`): surfaces are bodies of their own, and Booleans, fillets and shells refuse them (stitch them closed or thicken them). Offset and Thicken need a smooth surface (faces meeting at a crease are refused) and refit free-form surfaces to about 1e-7 of the size instead of offsetting them exactly. Patch is tangent only on four-sided boundaries and only along open edges, and fades near the corners; other boundaries are split about their centre with G0 seams. Trim cuts with a plane or one face and refuses a cut through a vertex; Extend works on edges that run along a parameter line and only continues the surface naturally; Match reshapes non-rational NURBS patches (Patch and Offset make them), exact in the middle of an edge and fading at its ends, G2 within 5 % in the test, not exact. Sewing is not shared with the IGES importer. Continuity is judged on shared edges only (stitch first). This is not a Class-A tool: no G3, no control-point editing.
